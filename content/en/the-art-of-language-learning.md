## Why Most People Struggle with Languages

Learning a new language is one of the most rewarding intellectual pursuits — yet most people give up within the first three months. The reason isn't lack of talent. It's lack of **system**.

### The Forgetting Curve

Hermann Ebbinghaus discovered in the 1880s that we forget roughly 70% of new information within 24 hours unless we actively review it. This is the core problem with traditional classroom learning.

The solution is **spaced repetition** — reviewing material at increasing intervals just before you're about to forget it.

### Immersion Techniques That Actually Work

- **Shadowing**: Listen to native audio and repeat simultaneously, matching rhythm and intonation
- **Extensive reading**: Read content slightly above your level — novels, news, blogs
- **Language exchange**: Platforms like Tandem or HelloTalk connect you with native speakers
- **Media immersion**: Switch your phone, OS, and streaming services to your target language

### The 80/20 Rule of Vocabulary

Research shows that the 1,000 most common words in any language cover roughly 85% of everyday speech. Focus there first.

| Vocabulary Size | Coverage |
|----------------|----------|
| 100 words | ~50% |
| 1,000 words | ~85% |
| 10,000 words | ~98% |

## Building a Sustainable Habit

Consistency beats intensity. Thirty minutes daily outperforms a four-hour weekend session every time. Use habit stacking — attach your language practice to an existing routine like morning coffee or your commute.

The polyglots who succeed aren't smarter. They've simply built systems that make practice inevitable.
