## The Reader Doesn't Owe You Attention

Every sentence you write is a small contract with the reader: "keep reading and I'll give you something valuable." Break that contract and they leave. The web makes this brutally visible — bounce rates don't lie.

### The Inverted Pyramid

Journalism figured this out a century ago. Put the most important information first:

1. **Lead**: What happened? Why does it matter?
2. **Body**: Supporting details, context, evidence
3. **Tail**: Background, history, tangential information

Readers who leave early still got the main point.

### Sentence Length Variation

Monotonous sentence length puts readers to sleep. Mix it up.

Short sentences punch. They create emphasis. They force the reader to pause.

Longer sentences, on the other hand, allow you to build context, connect related ideas, and guide the reader through more complex reasoning — but only when the complexity is earned.

### Active vs Passive Voice

```
Passive: The article was written by Elena.
Active:  Elena wrote the article.

Passive: Mistakes were made.
Active:  We made mistakes.
```

Active voice is shorter, clearer, and assigns responsibility.

### Cut Ruthlessly

Every word that doesn't earn its place weakens the ones that do:

```
Before: "In order to be able to achieve the goal of improving performance..."
After:  "To improve performance..."

Before: "It is important to note that..."
After:  (just say the thing)

Before: "Due to the fact that..."
After:  "Because..."
```

### Reading Level

Write at a reading level two grades below your audience's education. This isn't dumbing down — it's respecting their time. Even experts prefer clear prose.

The best writing feels effortless to read. That effortlessness costs the writer enormous effort.
